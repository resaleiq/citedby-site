Privacy

Last updated: 22 October 2026

This page explains what personal data CitedBy (citedby.eu) handles, why, on what legal basis, how long we keep it, who else receives it, and what you can do about it. It follows the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR).

3. Cookies

The public pages set no cookies and load no third-party scripts, and the analytics tag stores nothing on your device. We set a cookie only when a feature needs one. Each is strictly necessary, HttpOnly, sent over HTTPS only, and never used for tracking or advertising:

  • ci_session keeps you signed in. It is set when you sign in and lasts 30 days, or until you sign out.
  • ci_gstate is set only if you use "Continue with Google". It lasts 10 minutes and checks that the sign-in you return from is the one you started.
  • ci_share_<report id> is set when you open a password-protected shared report and enter its password. It lasts 24 hours so you do not retype the password.

Inside the signed-in app, your browser also keeps four display preferences in its local storage, not in a cookie: cb.pg.cols (which columns the page list shows), cb.pg.lim (how many rows it shows), cb.fixed (whether the setup checklist counts a fix as done) and cb.onb.hide (whether you closed the setup checklist). They hold no personal data, are never sent to us or anyone else, and you can clear them in your browser settings.

4. Who receives your data

We use service providers (processors), which handle data only on our instructions, and independent controllers, which use what they receive for their own purposes under their own privacy policies: Google (sign-in and Search Console), Microsoft (Bing Webmaster Tools and IndexNow) and the search engines that take part in IndexNow. Each receives only what its function needs.

  • Stripe Payments Europe Ltd (Ireland): payments, subscriptions, invoices and refunds. It receives your email address, plan and card details; card details never reach us. It may process data in the United States under Standard Contractual Clauses.
  • Resend (United States): sends our emails, so it receives your email address, the subject and the text of each message, including sign-in links, receipts, reports, digests and alerts. Standard Contractual Clauses.
  • Anthropic Ireland, Limited (Ireland, EU), which may transfer data to Anthropic, PBC (United States) under Standard Contractual Clauses: it receives the public text of the pages we crawled, so that we can assess each page and write the fixes. It does not receive your email address or account data.
  • OpenRouter (United States; Standard Contractual Clauses): receives general questions that we write ourselves about product categories, for our own reference data. It receives no email address, account data or audit content.
  • PostHog (EU cloud, Frankfurt, operated by PostHog Inc., United States; Standard Contractual Clauses): counts page visits and these steps. Our server passes it your IP address with each page view so it can tell visits apart for one day; it does not store the address. It also receives your browser's user-agent string (browser, operating system and device) with each event; it uses it with the IP address for the one-day count and stores neither. It stores the values listed in the visit counts row with each event, but no cookie, email address, name or account number, and no code that links your visits across days. It is not used when your browser sends Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control.
  • Forward Email (Forward Email LLC, United States; Standard Contractual Clauses): receives each message sent to hello@citedby.eu (sender address and message) and forwards it to the operator's Gmail inbox, provided by Google Ireland Ltd (EU), which may transfer data to Google LLC (United States) under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework.
  • Google Drive (Google Ireland Ltd, with Google LLC under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework): holds our nightly database backups (see the table above).
  • Hetzner Online GmbH (Germany; our servers are in Helsinki, Finland, EU): hosts our site and database.
  • Cloudflare, Inc. (United States, under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework): answers the DNS lookups we make when you verify a site by DNS record or check the custom domain for your branded reports. It receives that site address or domain and our server's IP address, and no account data.
  • Slack (Salesforce, Inc., United States, under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework): receives our project alerts (the audited site's address, its score, how many issues are new or fixed and what got worse, plus a link to the report) only at a Slack webhook address you give us in your project settings, so they go to your own Slack workspace under your agreement with Slack. With none set, nothing goes to Slack.
  • Microsoft Corporation (United States; Bing Webmaster Tools API, under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework): only if you connect Bing. On every check it receives the API key you gave us and your site's address, and returns what Bing holds about that site. It receives no other data about you.
  • IndexNow (api.indexnow.org, run by Microsoft Corporation, United States, under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework; an independent controller): only if you publish the IndexNow key file we give you and it checks out. After a crawl it receives your site's address, that key and the addresses of your new and changed public pages. IndexNow shares each submission with all the search engines that take part in it, which indexnow.org lists as Amazon, Bing, Naver, Seznam.cz, Yandex and Yep; each uses the addresses for its own index, as an independent controller. It receives no account data (no email address, name or payment details). The page addresses are public already, but if your site carries your name, or a page address names a person, the address can identify them. The legal basis is Art. 6(1)(b): you turn it on by publishing the key file, and removing the file stops every send; where a page address names someone else, Art. 6(1)(f), getting your public pages indexed. We keep the date of the last check and the date and size of the last send.
  • Google (Google Ireland Ltd, with Google LLC under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework) for the Search Console API: only if you connect Search Console. We keep the Google refresh token you grant, and on every check we send Google that token, the access token it yields and up to 5 page URLs of your site for index inspection. Google handles that data under its own privacy policy, as controller of your Search Console data.
  • Google (Google Ireland Ltd, with Google LLC under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework) is also an independent controller for the "Continue with Google" sign-in: it sees that you sign in to CitedBy and handles your Google account under its own privacy policy (policies.google.com/privacy), and it sends us your verified email address. We ask Google for your email address only (the openid and email scopes) and, for sign-in, keep no name, photo, contacts or Google token. For sign-in we send Google nothing else about you. The Search Console connection is separate and described above. Signing in with Google is optional; the email sign-in link works without it. The legal basis is Art. 6(1)(b).

We may also disclose data where the law requires it.

5. Transfers outside the EU

Where a recipient is in the United States (Resend, OpenRouter, PostHog Inc., Forward Email, Anthropic, Google, Cloudflare, Slack, Microsoft), the transfer rests either on the EU-US Data Privacy Framework (where we say so above) or on the Standard Contractual Clauses of the European Commission. Microsoft passes the addresses IndexNow receives on to the engines that take part; we have no agreement with them, and no safeguard of ours covers that sharing. Seznam.cz is in the EU (Czechia); Amazon is in the United States; Naver is in South Korea, covered by the European Commission's adequacy decision; Yep (Ahrefs Pte Ltd, Singapore) and Yandex (Russia) are in countries without an EU adequacy decision. If you don't want that, don't publish the key file, or remove it: we then send nothing to IndexNow. Write to hello@citedby.eu if you want a copy of the safeguards.

6. Deleting your account

You can export your account data and delete your account from your account settings. Deleting works in one action and takes effect at once:

  1. We first cancel every active subscription with Stripe. If that fails, we delete nothing and show you an error, so you are never billed for a deleted account. Try again, or write to us.
  2. Then we remove your account, your sign-in links, your address from our mail log, and everything your audits hold: audits, the pages we fetched, projects and their settings (including a Slack webhook address and verification token), the findings you marked as wrong, fix states and the verify timeline, Search Console figures, server-log counts, search engine connections (the stored Bing key and Google token) and the IndexNow check, brand settings, share passwords, schedules, and the team members you invited. An invitation another account sent to your address is removed too. Other accounts' data is never touched.

What stays after deletion, and why: the payment, invoice and withdrawal records described in the table above (six years, accounting law), and the one-trial record (Art. 6(1)(f)). Our database backups drop your data within 30 days, as the older copies are overwritten.

To have a single audit removed without deleting your account, write to hello@citedby.eu.

7. Your rights

Under the GDPR (Art. 15 to 22) you have the right to:

  • access your data and get a copy (Art. 15). You can download it from your account settings.
  • correct data that is wrong (Art. 16).
  • have your data erased (Art. 17), subject to the exceptions above.
  • restrict how we use it (Art. 18).
  • be told when we correct, erase or restrict data we shared (Art. 19).
  • receive it in a portable format (Art. 20): the export from your account settings is a JSON file.
  • object to processing that rests on our legitimate interest (Art. 21), for example the one-trial record, the abuse limits and the visit counts. We then stop unless we have compelling grounds or need the data for a legal claim.
  • not be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing with legal or similarly significant effects (Art. 22). We make no such decisions.

To use a right, write to hello@citedby.eu from the address your account uses. We reply within 2 working days and in any case complete your request within one month (Art. 12(3)). We may ask you to prove who you are.

8. Complaints

You have the right to complain to a data-protection authority. In Spain it is the Agencia Española de Protección de Datos (AEPD): aepd.es (complaints through its electronic office, sedeaepd.gob.es). You can also complain to the authority in the EU country where you live or work.
