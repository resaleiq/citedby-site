Does llms.txt work? What two studies and Google's own documentation say

By the CitedBy editorial team · Reviewed on 2026-10-18

Short answer

So far the evidence says llms.txt does not move AI citations. Ahrefs found 97% of valid llms.txt files got zero requests in May 2026, SE Ranking found no clear link to citations across about 300,000 domains, and Google says you do not need AI text files. Adding one is cheap, but do not expect traffic from it.

What llms.txt is

llms.txt is a proposal by Jeremy Howard, published in September 2024: a Markdown file at /llms.txt with a title, a short summary and lists of links, meant to give AI agents a concise map of a site (llmstxt.org). It is not an official standard of any search engine or AI lab. The proposal's own page lists adoption by documentation platforms and says OpenAI, Anthropic and Gemini publish the file for their own developer docs. That shows people write it. It does not show that assistants read yours when they answer a shopper.

What the data says

Does anything fetch it? Ahrefs checked 137,210 domains with its web analytics in May 2026. It found that 97% of valid llms.txt files received zero requests that month. Of the requests that did arrive, 19.5% came from named AI tools. SEO audit tools, unknown bots and general crawlers made up much of the rest. Ahrefs also reports that no AI bot went looking for the file on sites that did not have one (Ahrefs study).

Does it change citations? SE Ranking analysed about 300,000 domains and published in November 2025 that having llms.txt showed no clear connection to how often a domain is cited by AI, with 10.13% of domains having the file (Search Engine Journal on the SE Ranking study). Their own wording is that it "doesn't seem to directly impact AI citation frequency. At least not yet."

Both studies are correlation or log data from outside parties, run on different samples with different methods. They support "no measurable benefit so far", not "it can never matter".

What Google says

Google's documentation on AI features states that there are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, and that you do not need to create new machine-readable files, AI text files or markup to appear in them (Google Search Central: AI features). For ChatGPT, OpenAI documents the crawlers it uses (OAI-SearchBot, GPTBot, ChatGPT-User) and says robots.txt controls them; it does not describe llms.txt there (OpenAI crawler documentation).

Questions people ask

Does ChatGPT read llms.txt?

There is no published evidence that it does. OpenAI documents its crawlers and robots.txt but does not describe llms.txt, and Ahrefs found most requests to llms.txt files came from other kinds of bots.

Does llms.txt help me get cited by AI?

Not on current evidence. SE Ranking found no clear link across about 300,000 domains, and Google says no AI text file is needed for its AI features.

Can llms.txt hurt?

It can mislead if it is stale or lists pages you removed. Keep it short and accurate, or do not publish one.

Is llms.txt the same as robots.txt?

No. robots.txt tells crawlers what they may fetch. llms.txt is a proposed summary file for AI agents that no major search engine requires.
